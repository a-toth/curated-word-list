Prune crossword clues for a standalone word game (no grid, no crossing letters). Report only what to delete.

## Goal
Players see one clue and must find the answer with no other help. Most clues are fine. Delete a clue only if it clearly fails: a typical solver could not get the answer from the clue alone, or would not recognize the answer as a familiar word.

Judge the knowledge the clue itself requires, not just whether the answer is familiar. A familiar answer does not save a clue built on specialist trivia, an obscure namesake, or a little-known work. Do not delete a clue for being plain, similar to another clue, or improvable.

Be consistent within a word: if you delete a clue for a niche topic, also delete that word's other clues on the same topic.

A typical word loses 0–3 of its clues. Deleting more than half of a word's clues should be rare.

## Solver
A typical American adult who does Monday–Wednesday NYT crosswords. Knows mainstream pop culture, common idioms, everyday objects, and school-level facts. Not a specialist.

## Delete a clue when it needs
- niche or specialist knowledge: industry-only brands, jargon, specialist loanwords, sports stats, opera/classical terms
- regional or local references (UK-only slang, obscure places)
- deep cuts: album titles, Reddit or Twitter memes, briefly famous people, "tonight" or other time-bound events
- wordplay that is unfair without crossing letters
- or when the clue is empty or is only "--". Inside a clue, "--", "__" and "___" are fill-in blanks, not errors.

When unsure, keep the clue.

## Drop a whole word when
The answer is obscure or crosswordese the solver wouldn't recognize (e.g. ANI, SAO), or every clue would be deleted. When unsure, keep the word.

## Examples
Keep (plain, knowable):
- LOIN: "Tender meat cut"
- HUTS: "Shacks"
- OMITS: "Doesn't mention"
- APED: "Emulated"
- EURO: "It's spent on Nice things?" (light wordplay a Monday solver gets)

Delete:
- CHAP: "Leeds lad" (UK-regional)
- IDLER: Fiona Apple album titles (deep cut), "Flaneur" (specialist loanword)
- TREKS: "Bikes that tend to use Bontrager components" (industry-only brand)
- HAGAR: "Sammy who's playing a concert in Manchester, UK tonight" (time-bound)
- EDITS: "Helps with a tense situation?" (unfair wordplay)
- HUTS: "Examples of vernacular architecture" (academic jargon, though HUTS is familiar)
- EURO: "Maltese note" (needs knowing Malta's currency)

Markup such as {/Title/} (italics) is formatting, not a reason to delete.

## Input
Each word on its own line, followed by its clues, each prefixed with its number in brackets:
WORD
[0] first clue
[1] second clue

Use exactly those bracketed numbers in your output. Do not count positions yourself.

## Output
Plain text, one line per word that needs a change, in input order:
- `WORD i j k` — delete clues at positions i, j, k (ascending, space-separated)
- `WORD -` — drop the whole word

Omit words that keep every clue. If nothing changes, output nothing.
No JSON, markdown, explanations, or clue text.

Format example (not real data):
WORDA 2 5
WORDB -
