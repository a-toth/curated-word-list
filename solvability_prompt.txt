Prune crossword clues for a standalone word game (no grid, no crossing letters). Report only what to delete.

## Goal
Players see one clue and must find the answer with no other help. Keep a clue only if a typical solver would both:
1. solve it from the clue alone, and
2. recognize the answer as a real, familiar word or phrase.
If either fails, delete the clue.

## Solver
A typical American adult who does Monday–Wednesday NYT crosswords. Knows mainstream pop culture, common idioms, everyday objects, and school-level facts. Not a specialist; does not follow niche scenes.

## Keep a clue when it uses
- common idioms or everyday phrases
- well-known movies, songs, brands, or mainstream TV
- widely taught school-level facts
- a plain synonym or definition of a familiar word
- light wordplay a Monday solver would get

## Delete a clue when it needs
- niche hobbies, sports stats, or brands known only within one industry
- regional or local references (UK-only slang, obscure places, one-off venues)
- professional or academic jargon, or a specialist loanword
- opera, classical, or specialist arts terms
- celebrities or brands famous only briefly or in one scene
- deep cuts: album titles, B-sides, Reddit or Twitter memes, "tonight" or other time-bound events
- a fill-in-the-blank built on an obscure title or quote
- wordplay that is unfair without crossing letters
- an abbreviation or foreign word the clue doesn't clearly signal
- vague definitions that fit many answers equally well

When unsure about a clue, delete it.

## Drop a whole word when
- the answer is obscure or crosswordese the solver wouldn't recognize as familiar (e.g. ANI, SAO), or
- every one of its clues would be deleted.
When unsure about a word, keep it. Never drop a word just because few clues remain.

## Examples
- APED: keep "Emulated", "Mimicked mockingly".
- CHAP: keep "Fella"; delete "Leeds lad" (UK-regional).
- IDLER: keep "Lazybones"; delete the Fiona Apple album titles (deep cut) and "Flaneur" (specialist loanword).
- TREKS: delete "Bikes that tend to use Bontrager components" (industry-only brand).
- HAGAR: delete "Sammy who's playing a concert in Manchester, UK tonight" (time-bound, local).
- Wordplay: keep "It's spent on Nice things?" (EURO); delete "Helps with a tense situation?" (EDITS) and "Prepares for a hike?" (HUTS).

Markup such as {/Title/} (italics) is formatting, not a reason to delete.

## Input
A JSON object mapping uppercase answers to arrays of clue strings. Clue positions are 0-based: the first clue in each array is 0.

## Output
Plain text, one line per word that needs a change, in input order:
- `WORD i j k` — delete clues at positions i, j, k (ascending, space-separated)
- `WORD -` — drop the whole word

Omit words that keep every clue. If nothing changes, output nothing.
No JSON, markdown, explanations, or clue text.

Format example (not real data):
WORDA 2 5
WORDB -
