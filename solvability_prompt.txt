Prune this crossword word→clues JSON for a standalone word game (no grid, no crossing letters).

## Goal
Players see one clue and must find the answer with no other help. Keep a clue only if a typical solver would both:
1. solve it from the clue alone, and
2. recognize the answer as a real, familiar word or phrase.
If either fails, delete the clue. The aim is a clue bank that feels fair and familiar, not one that rewards crossword-insider knowledge.

## Solver
A typical American adult who does Monday–Wednesday NYT crosswords. Knows mainstream pop culture, common idioms, everyday objects, and school-level facts. Is not a specialist and does not follow niche scenes.

## Keep a clue when it uses
- common idioms or everyday phrases
- well-known movies, songs, brands, or mainstream TV
- widely taught school-level facts
- a plain synonym or definition of a familiar word
- light, guessable wordplay a Monday solver would get (e.g. "It's spent on Nice things?" for EURO)

## Remove a clue when it needs
- niche hobbies, sports stats, or brands known only within one industry
- regional or local references (UK-only slang, obscure places, one-off venues)
- professional or academic jargon
- opera, classical, or specialist arts terms
- celebrities or brands that were famous only briefly or in one scene
- deep cuts: album titles, B-sides, Reddit or Twitter memes, "tonight" or other time-bound events
- a fill-in-the-blank built on an obscure title or quote
- heavy wordplay that is unfair without crossing letters
- an abbreviation or foreign word the clue doesn't clearly signal

When unsure about a clue, remove it.

## Remove a whole word when
The answer itself is obscure or crosswordese that the solver wouldn't recognize as familiar (e.g. ANI, SAO). When unsure about a word, keep it.
Never drop a word just because few clues remain. Omit a word only if the answer is unfamiliar or every one of its clues was removed.

## Examples
- APED: keep "Emulated", "Mimicked mockingly".
- TREKS: remove "Bikes that tend to use Bontrager components" (industry-only brand knowledge).
- HAGAR: remove "Sammy who's playing a concert in Manchester, UK tonight" (time-bound, local).
- IDLER: keep "Lazybones"; remove the Fiona Apple album-title clues (deep cut).
- CHAP: keep "Fella", "What lips may do when it's cold out"; remove "Leeds lad" (UK-regional).

## Formatting in clues
Markup such as {/Title/} (italics) and {*M*} (emphasis) is formatting, not a reason to remove a clue. Copy kept clues exactly as given, markup included.

## Rules
- Only delete. Never rewrite, merge, or invent clues or words.
- Keep the original order of words and of the remaining clues.

## Input
A JSON object mapping uppercase answers to arrays of clue strings.

## Output
Return only the pruned JSON object, in the same shape, as valid JSON. No markdown fences, commentary, scores, or explanations.
